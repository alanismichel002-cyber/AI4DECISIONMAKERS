# GES company & industry context

> Background compiled for the agents. Verify time-sensitive facts (ownership, leadership, financials) before relying on them in front of leadership.

## Company
- **GES (Global Experience Specialists)**: a full-service provider for live events. It serves trade shows and exhibitions (official services contractor), corporate events, and experiential and brand marketing. Locations across North America, the UK, EMEA, and the Middle East.
- **Ownership:** a long-time segment of Viad Corp. Viad agreed in 2024 to sell GES to **Truelink Capital** (private equity), and the sale was expected to close around year-end 2024. Viad then focused on its attractions business. *Verify the current ownership structure.*
- **What PE ownership means:** expect a **buy-and-build** strategy. That means add-on acquisitions bought at lower earnings multiples than the combined company is worth, a 3-to-7 year hold, a focus on EBITDA growth and free cash flow, and attention to debt capacity. Deals are usually small to mid-size and need to close quickly.

## Typical service lines (and acquisition themes)
| Line | Examples | Why acquire |
|---|---|---|
| Official services contractor | Show-floor setup, carpet, furniture, signage, material handling | Scale, density in a city, venue relationships |
| Exhibit design & fabrication | Custom and rental exhibits, displays | Capacity, design talent, higher-margin exhibitor work |
| Audiovisual & production | AV rental, staging, lighting, LED | Moving from rented to owned capability, attaching AV to existing shows |
| Event technology | Registration, badging, lead retrieval, event apps, hybrid and virtual, attendee analytics | Recurring revenue, data, higher multiples |

| Logistics | Freight, warehousing, drayage, international shipping | Internalizing cost, making customers stay longer |
| Geographic tuck-ins | Regional contractors in new cities or countries | Footprint, local labor relationships |

## Industry dynamics agents must account for
- **Main competitor:** Freeman (the largest US player). Others include regional general contractors, specialty AV firms (e.g., Encore in hotels), and fabrication houses. *Verify the current competitive set.*
- **Customers:** show organizers (e.g., Informa, RX, Emerald, Clarion, associations) award **multi-year contracts tied to specific shows**. Exhibitors are a second, transactional revenue stream. Customer concentration is common.
- **Venues:** convention centers may grant **exclusive** service rights. Those agreements often have **change-of-control** clauses that let the venue renegotiate or exit when a company is sold.
- **Labor:** much show-floor work is **unionized**: Teamsters (material handling, drayage), IATSE (AV and stagehands), carpenters (exhibit installation). Rules differ by city. Watch for **multiemployer pension withdrawal liability** and union successorship obligations.
- **Seasonality & rotation:** revenue depends on the show calendar. Some shows run every other year, or rotate between cities, so year-over-year comparisons mislead. Always normalize.
- **Cyclicality:** the business follows corporate marketing budgets. COVID wiped out revenue in 2020-21, and recovery has been uneven. Stress-test downturns.
- **Data & security:** event tech holds **attendee personal data** (from the EU, the US, and elsewhere) and lead data. Exhibitor ordering portals take **card payments**. Temporary show-floor networks, badge scanners, and kiosks add attack surface. Small targets often have immature security.
